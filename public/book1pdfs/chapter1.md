The University of California lecture hall buzzes with a low hum of anticipation. Rows of students fill the tiered seats, their attention fixed on the professor pacing near the front as he delivers the final lecture of the semester. The clock strikes one in the afternoon, but in the very back row, one student isn’t paying attention.

Wesley Johnson sits reclined in his seat, his gaze distant—lost somewhere between the rafters and the clouds beyond the windows. His outfit catches a few glances on its own: a bright red mesh football-style jersey with the number “716” printed in large black lettering across the chest, paired with black cargo pants marked by distressed detailing and torn patches. His sneakers, vivid red to match the jersey, tap silently against the tile floor in a slow, thoughtless rhythm.

At the front of the room, Professor Fields’ voice dips into something more serious.

“I understand that today is particularly difficult for all of you,” he says, pausing with his hands clasped in front of him. “Today marks the third anniversary of the outbreak. Many of you have endured heartbreaking losses, and I recognize that such pain isn’t something one can simply overcome.”

His eyes drift across the hall. Most students remain silent, some nodding faintly. Wesley doesn’t move.

“I lost my own twin brother to that virus,” the professor continues. “And I can tell you—some days, it’s a struggle just to face the morning. But it’s crucial to persevere. Even when you feel alone, remember—there’s always someone to—”

A loud ringtone slices through the quiet.

Heads turn.

Wesley flinches, scrambling to silence his phone. His thumb fumbles across the screen, tapping the wrong button once before finally rejecting the call. He curses under his breath.

Professor Fields narrows his gaze.

“Mr. Johnson,” he says sharply, “do us all a favor and turn that phone off.”

“Sorry, Professor Fields,” Wesley mutters, eyes downcast.

The phone screen dims. One missed call: *Goldenboy.*

He exhales through his nose, drops the phone onto the desk in front of him, and leans back again, jaw tightening.

A nudge hits his arm. Samantha Clark, sitting beside him, leans over with a sly look in her eyes. “Who was that?”

Wesley cuts her a look—flat, unreadable—but the answer is written all over his face.

She grins. “Oh, your boyfriend?”

His jaw ticks. “For the last time,” he whispers, voice tight, “he’s not my boyfriend.”

A few students glance over at the sudden shift in tone.

Samantha lifts her hands in mock innocence, her smirk unshaken. “Nobody calls that many times unless they’re into you.”

Wesley turns back toward the front, shutting down the conversation with a low mutter. “We’re not having this conversation.”

Satisfied, she leans back, amusement still dancing in her eyes.

The heat of the California sun presses down on the pavement as Wesley and Samantha step out of the university building and into the afternoon bustle. The street is alive with movement—students fan out across the sidewalk, conversations drift past like static, and traffic groans in stop-and-go rhythm. Wesley barely registers any of it.

His steps are unhurried, hands tucked into the pockets of his black cargo pants. Each stride sends a subtle shift through the distressed fabric, frayed patches brushing against his knees. His bright red sneakers flash against the concrete with each step, matching the bold mesh football-style jersey stretched across his chest—its front stamped with a large black “716.” In the sunlight, the golden warmth in his honey-bronze skin catches just beneath his cheekbones and across his forehead, softening the edge of his otherwise guarded expression.

Beside him, Samantha’s face lights up as she catches sight of someone across the courtyard. “Angie\!”

She pulls a girl into a tight hug, laughing as they rock back and forth.

“I’m good\!” Angelina says, returning the smile. “What about you? I haven’t seen either of you since Spring Break.”

“Yeah,” Samantha groans. “From the second we got back, it’s been nonstop. Barely enough time to breathe. But I’ve been good.”

Angelina chuckles, brushing a lock of hair behind her ear. “Trust me, I get it.”

Then her eyes slide past Samantha and land on Wesley—who lingers just behind, quiet, his head tilted slightly. His gaze is fixed on something far off. A bus whooshes past on the road beside them, and as it rolls by, his eyes follow it, reflecting the motion in a glassy, unfocused stare. It’s not until the engine noise fades that he seems to return to the moment.

“How about you, Wes?” Angelina asks, her voice softening.

Samantha glances back at him and catches the trace of distance still in his eyes. He doesn’t answer.

“Earth to Wes?”

He blinks, then shifts his weight. “Sorry. What’d you say?”

“I asked how you are,” Angelina says with a raised brow, “but I think I already know.”

Samantha jabs a thumb his way, her voice light. “He’s avoiding a certain ‘friend’ who won’t stop calling.”

Wesley’s jaw tightens. His eyes narrow slightly as he turns to her—slow and precise.

“We’re not doing this again,” he says. His voice is low and clipped, not loud but sharp enough to cut through the air. “Just because I don’t talk to anyone doesn’t mean I’m secretly seeing someone. And even if I was—it’s not your business.”

His gaze shifts to Angelina, the edge in his voice receding just enough to sound civil. “But yeah. I’m fine. Thanks for asking.”

The bite in his tone catches her off guard. A few students passing nearby slow their steps, eyes briefly flicking toward the tension hanging between the three of them.

Angelina raises her hands in mock surrender. “I would ask for more details… but I think I’m good. So—are you guys hungry?”

Samantha immediately rubs her stomach. “Yes. I’m starving. Our last class was three hours long, and he didn’t even let us out early.”

Angelina smirks. “Damn. Okay. Wes, you in?”

Wesley hesitates. His eyes drift down the block, unfocused again. Then he nods once.

“Yeah. Sure.”

Angelina’s eyebrows rise. “Really? Okay. I know a spot close by.”

They fall into step together, weaving through the crowd.

“So,” Angelina asks, “any plans for summer break?”

“Actually, yeah,” Samantha says.

Angelina’s eyes light up. “Spill.”

“I’m flying back to New York tonight.”

Wesley looks over, faint interest rising in his expression. “You heading up to Baxter? To see Ciara and Kylie?”

“Yeah. They’ve got two weeks left, so I figured I’d crash at my sister’s for a few days, then train up to Connecticut.”

“Visiting friends,” Angelina says with a nod. “Solid move.”

Samantha shoots Wesley a look. “You know they haven’t seen you since we moved out here.”

“I’m not doing the back-and-forth thing,” Wesley replies. His voice flattens. “I live here now. I’m not dragging myself across the country unless I absolutely have to.”

Angelina gives him a side glance. “So what are you doing, then?”

He opens his mouth. “I’m ac—”

His phone buzzes.

Wesley pulls it from his pocket. *Goldenboy.*

He stares at the screen for a second longer than he needs to. Then he locks it and shoves it away.

Samantha frowns. “You can’t keep ignoring him, you know.”

The phone buzzes again.

A message lights up:  
 **You can’t keep ignoring me.**

Wesley exhales slowly. “He seems to think the same thing,” he mutters.

Samantha catches it. “Then why won’t you just answer?”

They stop at a red light. Wesley crosses his arms, gaze fixed straight ahead, unmoving.

“I have my reasons.”

His eyes flick between them, then settle on Samantha.

“What I don’t get,” he says, “is why you can’t just let it go.”

Angelina cuts in quickly. “Hey. Sam’s just trying to help.”

“Yeah,” Wesley says, still watching her. “And I don’t remember asking for help.”

“Wes…”

But he’s already stepping back, his posture folding inward, closing the door.

“You know what? You guys go ahead. I’m going home.”

“Wes, wait—” Angelina reaches out, but he’s already turning away.

He walks fast, head low, weaving through students without looking back. His red sneakers vanish into the crowd.

By the time he’s two blocks away, the noise fades. His phone vibrates again in his pocket. He doesn’t check it.

They don’t get it. Not really.

And they can’t.

Not yet.

It’s been three years since the day everything broke apart. Since Iztan. Since Azazel. Since the team fractured for good.

They blame him.

And maybe… they’re right.

After the dust settled, his father offered him something bigger: a leadership position in S.T.A.T.I.C., his own team, real authority. Wesley turned it down. He wanted to disappear. To live like none of it ever happened.

And for a while, he almost did.

Then, a year ago, S.T.A.T.I.C. fell. Noxus burned it down from the inside. His father vanished. Gavin went quiet.

Until today.

And now the silence is broken.

An hour passes before Wesley returns to his loft.

Outside the door, he taps out a quick text to Samantha and Angelina:  
 **I’m fine. Sorry for snapping at you both. I’ll call you later.**

He slips the phone into his pocket, unlocks the door, and steps inside. The space greets him with its usual stillness, but something tugs at his senses. As he pushes the door shut behind him, the hairs on his arms rise.

He sighs. “You know, just because I don’t answer my phone doesn’t mean you get to break into my home.”

Wesley flicks on the lights—and sure enough, Gavin is already seated on the couch like he owns the place.

Wesley’s gaze travels up from the familiar black and white Converse on Gavin’s feet to the brown jeans creased at the knee, the tan T-shirt tucked neatly beneath a matching stone-colored shacket. But it’s Gavin’s face that gives him pause: lean and sharp-edged, pale skin cool-toned and slightly sun-starved. A dusting of scruff lines his jaw and upper lip, barely hiding the stress written in the faint shadows beneath his icy blue eyes.

His blonde hair—grown long and wavy—falls forward in loose strands, some of it brushing just above his brows. The tips are sun-kissed, though faded now from months spent off the grid. That signature curve of a smirk tugs faintly at one corner of his mouth, more flicker than grin.

“You done?” Gavin asks, his voice smooth and unbothered.

Wesley rolls his eyes, folding his arms across his chest. He’s in no mood to entertain Gavin’s usual charisma.

“How’d you know I was here?” Gavin continues, still lounging like this is a casual visit.

“Did you forget what my powers are or something?” Wesley says, flat.

Gavin pauses, clearly drawing a blank for a beat before the realization dawns. “Right. It’s been a while.”

“Yeah. I’ve been busy.”

Gavin hears the coldness, but he doesn’t blink. “You’ve been avoiding my calls all morning.”

“Oh, trust me. I’m aware.”

As Wesley strolls across the loft, Gavin watches him carefully. There’s irritation in his stance now—the kind that simmers just under control. Wesley’s deliberate pace only makes it worse.

“You’ve been blowing up my phone for hours,” Wesley says over his shoulder. “And clearly, you don’t know when to quit. Personally, if someone ignored all my texts and calls, I’d take the hint. Which is—”

“‘Busy,’” Gavin interrupts, standing now. His posture is poised—measured. Calm, but not passive.

“Yeah. I got the message, Wes. I just didn’t care.”

Wesley turns with a blank stare. “So?”

“You serious?” Gavin’s tone finally cracks, not angry, just... disappointed. “Wes, I’ve always been there for you. And now you’re icing me out when you know damn well it’s not easy to check in—especially when we’re still in hiding.”

“That’s just it, Gavin. I wouldn’t know anything about that.”

Wesley walks past him, shrugs off his backpack and tosses it on the chair near the dining table. His face stays unreadable, but Gavin doesn’t miss the tension beneath it.

“It’s been two years since I heard from you. Or my dad. So don’t act like you get to be mad at me. You’re living your life,” Wesley says, voice flat, “and I’m living mine.”

Gavin scoffs. “I get it.”

Wesley raises an eyebrow. “Get what?”

“I thought you were pissed at me. But you’re still haunted.”

Wesley stiffens. Crosses his arms again.

“What are you talking about?”

“You know exactly what I’m talking about,” Gavin says. His voice dips lower—still steady, but it carries weight. “You cut everyone out. You buried everything and everyone connected to that day. And for what? To pretend it never happened?”

Gavin shakes his head. “You need to realize it wasn’t your fault. ‘Cause whatever this version of you is—it’s not you, man. It’s bitter. And honestly? It’s exhausting.”

Wesley doesn’t move. But the flash of something across his face—shock, maybe—betrays the blow.

“I’m sorry,” Gavin says, backing off. “That’s not how I meant that.”

“Save it.”

Their eyes lock—Wesley’s unreadable and worn; Gavin’s still steady but pulled taut with guilt.

“I don’t want your apology,” Wesley says. “Just tell me why you’re here. Because the last thing I need is you, of all people, telling me how to live.”

Wesley turns his back again, going through the mail on his table.

“Wes—”

“Just tell me why you came,” Wesley says, not looking up.

Gavin draws a breath, lets the tension fall away for now. “Okay. I’m here because we have a lot to talk about. Starting with your dad.”

That makes Wesley pause. He flicks the envelope in his hand back onto the table.

“What about him?”

“He… no—we’ve been keeping things from you.”

Wesley doesn’t even blink. “What else is new?”

“Could you just look at me for a second?”

Wesley sighs. Slowly, he turns.

And sees the object in Gavin’s hand.

A small cube glows in Gavin’s palm, pulsing with a faint white light. It catches on his pale skin, the shimmer of it reflecting in his sharp jawline and messy blonde hair. The glow illuminates the space between them, quiet and humming.

Wesley’s eyes narrow. “What am I looking at?”

“It’s a holocube,” Gavin says. “It has everything you need to know.”

He takes a step forward. Then another. The distance between them disappears fast—but there’s no threat in the way Gavin moves. Just quiet urgency.

Wesley doesn’t move, but he tilts his head slightly as Gavin stops just a few feet away.

Waiting.

Then, without hesitation, Gavin turns and releases his grip on the holocube.

It hits the floor with a soft clack and rolls a few inches before coming to a halt, humming faintly. A moment later, projections spring to life—holographic images shimmer into the air, flickering in pale blue as they stabilize. What emerges is a surreal cascade of stolen security footage, blueprints, and brief flashes of combat.

Wesley’s eyes lock onto the moving display.

Gavin speaks without looking away. “For the past year we’ve been getting reports of Noxus stealing equipment. First Omega Labs. Then Dynamic Industries. Last week, P.A.R.A.G.O.N.”

Wesley turns toward him, brow furrowed. “Why are they robbing a lab, a billion-dollar company, and a top-secret government facility?”

Gavin meets his gaze. “Because Noxus is just a cover. The real organization behind it is worse.”

His voice tightens with the last word, and for a second, his icy blue eyes flicker—uneven, uncertain.

Wesley catches it. That momentary hesitation is all he needs.

“It’s Iztan. Isn’t it?”

Gavin’s eyes widen slightly. “You don’t seem very shocked.”

Wesley shifts his focus back to the holocube, watching the footage of men in full black tactical gear. “I saw rumors on the dark web a few months after I moved here. But nothing after that. So I let it go.”

Gavin gapes. “Why didn’t you tell me? Also—why are you on the dark web?”

“That’s none of your business. I was trying to move on. The chatter didn’t seem credible, and I figured we’d crushed them. Did we not?”

“We did,” Gavin says. “But the problem is, we don’t know what they’re building with the stolen tech. Or why. Your dad’s stretched thin. That’s why I came to you.”

Wesley crosses his arms. “Why me?”

“Because besides your dad and mine, you’re the only person alive who understands Iztan from the inside. What they’re capable of.”

Wesley doesn’t speak. His jaw tenses.

Brief flashes cross his mind—too fast to stop. A hospital bed. A man on life support. A woman’s lifeless body cradled in his arms. He blinks them away.

“Their leader’s dead,” he says at last. “So who’s pulling the strings?”

Gavin taps his wrist device. New footage overlays the projection—grainy security cam clips from Omega Labs. Rows of black-armored soldiers march through frame. But then—something different. A woman steps into view, unmasked, composed, distinct.

“These were pulled from Omega Labs' surveillance,” Gavin says.

Wesley’s eyes track her. She moves with purpose, slicing through the frame. Then—unexpected—she stops. Glances back toward the camera.

Wesley’s jaw locks.

“You can’t be serious.”

He looks at Gavin, doesn’t even need to ask.

“Celeste Masters,” Gavin confirms. “She’s been calling the shots the whole time. Noxus. The fall of S.T.A.T.I.C. All of it.”

Wesley takes a step back, rubbing at the nail of his ring finger—an old habit.

“I wanted to tell you,” Gavin adds quickly, “but your dad convinced me it was better not to. He thought—”

Wesley whirls on him, voice rising fast.

“This is exactly the kind of situation where you should involve me\! It took everything we had to beat them last time\!”

His voice drops again, heavy. “And even then, so many lives were lost.”

Gavin draws in a slow breath. “Your dad said it was better to let you enjoy the life you built. He didn’t want to drag you back in.”

Wesley’s voice sharpens. “And I get that. I do. But everyone always thinks they know what’s best for me. This—this right here—is why I didn’t join S.T.A.T.I.C. in the first place.”

He studies Gavin’s face, noting the guilt threading through his jawline and furrowed brow. The confident facade frays slightly.

“What even made you show up?” Wesley asks.

“The most recent robbery,” Gavin says. “P.A.R.A.G.O.N. Last week.”

He swipes through the holocube’s interface. “The team and I intercepted Iztan operatives—Jade, Keith, Danny and me—but we got ambushed by a second group.”

As he speaks, more images appear. Wesley steps beside him now, eyeing the footage.

Six figures. One, unmistakably Celeste.

“Great,” Wesley mutters. “She’s assembled a team.”

“Yeah,” Gavin says. “Aiden, Talia, Enzo, Nolan—and one guy I still can’t identify.”

He gestures to a figure in tactical gear and an oni mask, moving with eerie precision.

Wesley narrows his eyes. “Never seen or heard of him. Besides your brother, none of them were on my radar.”

“Same,” Gavin says, voice dark. “But they’re all ex-S.T.A.T.I.C.”

“You’re joking.”

“I wish.”

He lists them off: “Aiden has light manipulation, like me. Enzo can grow or shrink his size—he’s brutal up close. Talia can self-replicate. Nolan’s a speedster.”

Wesley frowns. “And they’re working for Celeste? A Deviant-hating psychopath?”

“They’re not like us. They’re Unnaturals.”

Wesley blinks. “Unnaturals?”

“They got their powers the way Azazel did. Artificially. We just gave it a name this time.”

Wesley’s jaw tightens. “So… Celeste has powers now too?”

“Multiple,” Gavin says. “She’s got ergokinesis—she can manipulate Deviant energy. Drain it, steal it, weaponize it. And she has teleportation. And telepathy.”

Wesley rubs the back of his neck. “She’s an Alpha now?”

“Not just her. She upgraded them too. All of them. Modified to Alpha-class levels.”

“You can’t be serious,” Wesley mutters. “You’ve been the strongest Deviant alive for years. And she just made a clone squad?”

Gavin gives a bitter laugh. “Insane, right?”

Wesley points to the masked man. “What about him?”

Gavin’s expression hardens. “He’s the most dangerous person I’ve ever fought. No powers that explode or burn—but he’s perfect. Reflexes. Aim. Tactics. Every move is surgical. Immune to energy attacks, maybe more. I thought I could take him and Aiden.”

Wesley stares. “Why would you think that was a good idea?”

“I didn’t know,” Gavin says. “And Aiden’s still better with his powers than I am.”

He plays more footage. Wesley watches silently as the masked man dances around Gavin’s attacks—dodging fusion blasts, countering with brutal efficiency.

Wesley’s eyes narrow. “This guy… I’ve heard whispers. Someone fitting that description came up months ago.”

Gavin raises an eyebrow.

Wesley shrugs. “Just because I’m not in the field doesn’t mean I stopped watching.”

Gavin looks at him closely now—really looks.

The sleeve of Wesley’s jersey is pushed up just slightly, and Gavin spots it: a pale pockmark near his inner elbow. Another faint line near his wrist. Marks most people would overlook.

But not Gavin.

His gaze lingers, eyes softening.

Wesley notices. “What?”

“Nothing,” Gavin says. But he doesn’t look away. “Just… sometimes I forget how much you’ve already been through.”

Wesley doesn’t respond. Not with words. But for a moment, his posture eases. Just a little.

Then the holocube flickers again—and the moment passes.

Wesley steps back from the holocube, his mind spinning, and crosses the loft toward the dining table. He rummages through his backpack and pulls out a sleek, black laptop—military-grade, no manufacturer branding, custom-forged by someone who doesn’t trust big corporations.

Gavin glances over his shoulder from the holographic display, then double-takes when he sees the device.

Wesley drops into the chair, flips open the screen with practiced ease, and types in his password. The desktop background loads: a photo of a younger Wesley and Gavin at a pool, Wesley laughing from Gavin’s shoulders, both of them grinning like nothing could touch them.

Gavin’s smirk softens. That memory still glows somewhere in him.

But then the image flickers to the desktop—another photo. A family portrait. Wesley, his mom, dad, and uncle, standing beside a Christmas tree. All smiles. Frozen in time.

Gavin’s expression darkens. He clears his throat, as if to wash away the sting.

“What are you looking for?” he asks.

“Answers,” Wesley says simply.

The rhythmic tap of keys fills the silence. Gavin walks over, peering past Wesley’s shoulder.

“Here,” Wesley mutters.

Gavin leans in as Wesley clicks into an open news tab. A headline leaps off the screen:

**“Unidentified Attacker Raids Frasier Industries Headquarters in New York—Dozens Dead.”**

“Two months ago,” Wesley says, “your mystery guy hit Frasier Industries.”

Gavin furrows his brow. “Frasier... why does that sound familiar?”

Wesley turns to him, incredulous. “Ramesh Frasier? Billionaire? Owns half of Manhattan?”

Gavin blinks. “As in one of the richest men alive? That Frasier?”

Wesley sighs. “Yes. I was friends with his son. Justin Frasier. Remember?”

“Vaguely.”

Wesley turns back to the screen, his expression slowly tightening. The details unfold in grim clarity.

“Christ,” he mutters.

“What?”

“He murdered a bunch of cops—including the chief of police.”

Gavin winces. “Damn.”

“There’s still nothing on his name or alias,” Wesley continues, scrolling.

“Seriously?”

“Give me a second.”

Gavin watches as Wesley’s fingers tear across the keyboard with near-mechanical speed. His brows rise as tab after tab opens, article after article loads.

“Jesus,” Gavin mutters, watching. “You type faster than I swipe.”

As the barrage slows, dozens of windows remain open, displaying image after image: bodies, shattered labs, burned insignias, news clips, classified leaks.

“His kill count is unreal,” Wesley says. “And not random either—politicians, federal agents, high-level contractors. All hits for money. Big money. Which means Iztan has a serious stockpile of funds somewhere.”

Gavin exhales. “And we didn’t know about this... why?”

“Because you don’t dig like I do,” Wesley says. “I’m running one of my tracking algorithms across the open web, dark web, private dumps—you wouldn’t have even linked these jobs if you didn’t know what to look for.”

Gavin leans closer. “You find anything else?”

“Yeah.” Wesley pauses. “A few encrypted forums talk about his kills. They keep mentioning one name: Shi.”

Gavin bursts into laughter. Wesley swivels in his chair to face him, confused.

“What’s so funny?” he asks, eyeing his friend.

Gavin wipes away a tear, still chuckling. “It’s just... what kind of name is Shi?”

Wesley gives him a look—half smirk, half condescension.

“You know Shi is Japanese for death, right?”

Gavin freezes mid-wipe. His smile fades instantly. “You’re kidding.”

Wesley doesn’t blink.

“Since when do you know Japanese?” Gavin asks.

“I’ve picked up a few things over the years,” Wesley says, turning back to the laptop.

A new image appears—blades. Twin double-edged weapons, slightly curved and gleaming with a faint sheen. The file names suggest toxicity. Wesley clicks through close-ups.

“Also,” he adds, “Shi carries two of these. Both laced with poison.”

Gavin’s silence speaks volumes.

Wesley doesn’t miss it. “What aren’t you telling me?”

Gavin straightens slightly. “What do you mean?”

Wesley places a firm hand on Gavin’s wrist. His fingers press in—subtle, gentle. A faint vibration pulses from his palm into Gavin’s arm. Wesley tunes into it like a stethoscope, letting it ride the pathways of blood and bone. He feels the rhythm shift. Gavin’s heart thumps faster—irregular, anxious.

Wesley’s eyes narrow. “You know how I feel about lying. Cut it out.”

Gavin jerks his arm away. “Jade got hurt.”

Wesley’s breath hitches. His posture slumps for half a second, the deflection gone.

“What?”

Gavin grabs a nearby chair, dragging it next to Wesley. He drops into the seat, his usual composure stripped down to something rawer.

“There was so much blood,” Gavin says quietly.

He looks at his hands—clean now—but for a moment, his eyes glaze, reliving the crimson.

“What happened?” Wesley asks.

“That assassin. Shi. And Aiden. Had me cornered. Jade backed me up—took one of those blades to the side.”

Wesley glances back at the image of the weapons, something hard tightening behind his eyes.

“He almost got away,” Gavin says, jaw clenched. “Until Keith caught up and… burned off both his arms.”

Wesley stares. “Wait—what?”

“Yeah,” Gavin nods. “Keith’s a little... intense. But the real problem is the poison. It’s screwing with Jade’s powers. Her healing isn’t working.”

Wesley doesn’t respond right away. His gaze falls. Concern colors his face—genuine, deep.

“She’ll be fine,” Gavin adds, gently. “Valeria’s working on something. But it might be a while before Jade’s able to fight. Or even walk again.”

Relief washes over Wesley—enough to ease the tightness in his shoulders—but the storm behind his eyes doesn’t clear.

“Is everyone else okay?” he asks.

“Banged up,” Gavin replies. “But they’ll make it. And you know I don’t bruise easily.”

Wesley sighs. “Gav… I wish you had told me sooner.”

Gavin looks over at him. “Yeah,” he says, quieter this time. “I wish I did too.”

A long silence follows—just the hum of the holocube, and the soft glow of weapons and faces suspended in midair.

Wesley glances back at the floating projections, his eyes narrowing on a set of unfinished schematics flickering along the periphery of the holocube’s display. Something about the design snags at his attention—an itch of recognition building at the back of his mind.

“Have you guys figured out what they’re trying to build?” he asks, his tone sharp, but calm.

Gavin follows his line of sight. “We think it’s some kind of weapon. But we’re not sure.”

Wesley stares at the schematics, his gaze dissecting every inch. The shapes, the inputs, the power matrix—it’s not unfamiliar. He leans forward slightly, tension creeping into his shoulders.

“No,” he says under his breath.

Gavin furrows his brow. “No? What do you mean ‘no’?”

Wesley rises, crossing to the projection and examining the display more closely. “I mean you’re wrong. It’s not a weapon. It’s something far worse.”

The holographic light washes over him, casting soft blue shadows across his brown skin and sharpening the edge in his expression.

“I’ve seen these schematics before,” he says.

He turns on his heel and heads back to the dining table, his fingers already tapping across his laptop. He presses three keys in quick succession. The desktop interface vanishes, replaced with a secondary system. Dozens of folders snap into view. He navigates with surgical precision.

Gavin watches from behind, his eyes pausing on one particular folder: Iztan.

Wesley clicks into it, types in a password, then scrolls until he lands on a file labeled simply: Resurrection.

He opens it.

On screen, the same schematics appear—but complete. Older. A finalized version of the incomplete render in the holocube.

Gavin stares. “Wait—what is this?”

Wesley doesn’t look up. “It’s a chamber. Designed to bring people back from the dead. I saw it in one of Iztan’s labs years ago. I stole the blueprint. S.T.A.T.I.C. tested it… called it a failure.”

The silence that follows is thick—until Wesley suddenly freezes.

His entire frame tenses. His head lifts slightly.

“Oh crap…”

Gavin’s expression tightens. “What?”

Wesley’s eyes drift toward him, panic beginning to stir. “There’s only one person they’d want to bring back with this.”

Gavin watches as Wesley’s hand finds the nail of his ring finger, rubbing it anxiously.

Then it happens—quick and visceral.

A rush of images assaults Wesley’s mind: a man gasping through tubes in a hospital bed… a woman, dead in his arms… a young man falling into flame. The grief hits like a wave. The ground beneath them starts to tremble, furniture rattling with the force of a vibration bleeding from Wesley’s body.

“Woah\!” Gavin steps forward fast. “Hey—hey, relax.”

He places a hand on Wesley’s shoulder.

“I’m here. Deep breaths, Wes.”

Wesley inhales—slow, shaky. The vibration softens. His eyes close for a moment as he tilts his head back, jaw tight. A flicker of embarrassment cuts through the tension.

“Sorry,” he says, opening his eyes. “That… doesn’t usually happen anymore.”

“I know,” Gavin replies quietly. “And it takes a hell of a lot to push you that far. Which means you were only thinking about one person.”

Wesley doesn’t deny it.

“That’s exactly who I’m thinking of.”

His gaze finds Gavin’s—and for a second, they don’t need words. Wesley’s deep brown eyes carry all the weight Gavin needs to read.

“Azazel?” Gavin says.

Wesley nods. Once. Solid. Sure.

Gavin steps back like he’s been slapped. “No. That’s impossible.”

“Is it?” Wesley’s voice is quiet, but it lands like steel. “Who else would they do all this for?”

The question hangs in the air like smoke—heavy, undeniable.
